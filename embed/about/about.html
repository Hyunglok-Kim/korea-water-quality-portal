Three vantage points

One water cycle, seen from orbit, from the air and from the ground

Most of our work starts with a measurement. Satellites cover the globe every few days, drones map a field at one meter, and sensors in the soil report every five minutes. We work where the three meet.

  • Orbit
    33 satellitesThe Earth-observing missions we follow: microwave radiometers and radars for soil moisture and floods, optical and thermal imagers for vegetation and water, and missions that measure rain, rivers and groundwater. The globe shows where each of them is now.
  • Air
    1 m mapsDrone flights over our field site every one to two weeks in season: true color, NDVI, thermal, LiDAR and an L-band radiometer.
  • Ground
    47 stations, 82 sensorsSoil moisture and temperature every 5 minutes since June 2025, in one 1 km grid cell at Hampyeong: South Korea’s first Core Validation Site, built to check the soil moisture maps of NASA SMAP and NASA-ISRO NISAR.

About HydroAI

AI for observing, understanding and predicting the Earth system

The Hydrological Remote Sensing and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory (HydroAI) combines microwave UAV and satellite observations, numerical models, and state-of-the-art machine learning and foundation model techniques to address environmental change, natural hazards, and water resource challenges driven by climate change and human activities.

We ask how the water held in soil and vegetation, falling as rain and carried by rivers, responds as the climate changes, from a single field to the whole globe, and we turn the answers into datasets and forecasts.

The lab is led by Prof. Hyunglok Kim, who joined GIST’s Department of Environment and Energy Engineering in 2023. Meet the people →

Research along two main axes

  • Axis 1Domain-informed AI modelingThe physics of hydrology and remote sensing, brought together with deep learning, data assimilation and foundation models, to predict droughts, floods, wildfires, dust storms and changes in water resources quickly and accurately.
  • Axis 2Earth foundation modelsNext-generation AI models, built on satellite big data and large-scale pretraining, that generalize across environmental problems: beyond task-specific models, toward AI that understands the Earth.

This work underpins climate change response, disaster management and data-driven environmental policy, and it advances AI methodology itself, especially the use of foundation models in science. See our current projects →

How we work

From a measurement to something others can use

  1. 01Observe

    Satellite records, drone flights and our own ground network. Where the record we need does not exist yet, we build it, and we check it against the ground.

  2. 02Model

    Land surface models simulate how water and energy move between soil, vegetation and the atmosphere. Data assimilation brings the simulation and the observations together.

  3. 03Learn

    Deep learning and foundation models, trained on years of satellite and model data, learn what the equations leave out, and we ask each model how uncertain it is.

  4. 04Share

    Papers, open datasets with a portal of their own, code, and the courses we teach at GIST.

Teaching and training

Courses at GIST, a weekly journal club, and students on the conference stage

We teach deep learning for environmental big data, microwave remote sensing with AI, and statistics for environmental and energy sciences. The lab meets every week for a journal club. Undergraduates join through Open Lab days and a four-week internship, and graduate students present their work every year at meetings such as AGU, EGU, AOGS and KWRA.
