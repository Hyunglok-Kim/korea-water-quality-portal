When to use this data

Where the monitoring network has no point. South Korea's national network measures water quality at fixed points, and at reservoirs on a schedule of about four visits a year. This dataset covers the water between them: on every clear Sentinel-2 overpass from 2016 to 2024 it gives six parameters for each 1 km cell whose water surface the satellite saw. Use it to compare one water body with another, to map which reservoirs and river reaches run turbid, green or nutrient-rich, to find where conditions were elevated on a given date, and, for water temperature and dissolved oxygen, as a series at a fixed place. It sits on a regular grid that aggregates onto ERA5-Land and other hydro-climate data, and every value carries an uncertainty for weighting or filtering. It does not replace in-situ measurement: the grids locate elevated conditions well but understate how elevated they were. What the paper shows →

What is new

Six parameters on one national grid, each with a calibrated uncertainty. Water temperature, suspended solids, chlorophyll-a, dissolved oxygen, total nitrogen and total phosphorus, including the nutrients and oxygen that have no colour of their own, are estimated together for the inland waters of mainland South Korea and released as an analysis-ready grid. One neural network per parameter learns from 46,933 matchups between national monitoring records and Harmonized Landsat and Sentinel-2 reflectance, ERA5-Land weather, terrain and land cover. It is applied at 30 m and averaged to 1 km only where at least 80 % of a cell's water was seen; nothing is gap-filled. The paper also states what each layer can and cannot carry. How it works →

From the paper

How the grids are made, and how far to trust them — in plain words

A satellite sees the colour of water, and colour says something about what is in it: sediment brightens it, algae turn it green. Weather, terrain and the land around a water body say more. The dataset joins the two with national monitoring records, and is open about which of the six layers the satellite really informs. The six figures below are the manuscript's own.

1 · From monitoring records to a national grid

Measurements of the national monitoring network (WEIS) were paired with the satellite view of the same water taken within three days, which gave 46,933 matchups at 1,194 monitoring points (235 river, 793 reservoir, 166 lake and dam points). Each matchup has 63 inputs: the mean and spread of reflectance in ten bands of the Harmonized Landsat and Sentinel-2 product (HLS S30) over a 7 × 7 window of open-water pixels, and the number of such pixels; four spectral indices; twenty ERA5-Land weather and land-surface fields; elevation and local relief; the land cover within 500 m and 2 km; the day of the year; and the days between overpass and sample.

One multi-layer perceptron per parameter learns from these. It is then run on every 30 m water pixel of every scene, and the 30 m values are averaged into a 1 km cell only where at least 80 % of the cell's water, and at least five pixels, had a valid prediction. Every other cell stays empty for that date. The result is one grid per clear overpass, not one per day.

2 · Against measurements at stations the model never saw

The skill is measured by holding whole stations out, five folds in turn. Water temperature is reproduced well (R² 0.87, RMSE 2.6 °C) and dissolved oxygen moderately (R² 0.53, RMSE 1.8 mg/L). For suspended solids, chlorophyll-a, total nitrogen and total phosphorus R² is 0.29 to 0.36 in concentration units and 0.50 to 0.57 on the logarithmic scale they were trained on.

All six pull extremes toward the middle, as any model that predicts an average does. At the top one percent of the observations the predictions recover 0.83 of the departure from typical conditions for water temperature, and 0.36 to 0.58 for the other five. A cell is a sound central estimate; it understates how far an event went.

3 · Three kinds of layer

The inputs each network leans on show what it is really doing. Suspended solids, chlorophyll-a and total phosphorus are optical retrievals: they are led by reflectance (blue and green for suspended solids, the chlorophyll index NDCI and the red edge for chlorophyll-a, blue and the red edge for total phosphorus). Total nitrogen is a catchment-context estimate: it is led by the water and wetland fractions of its surroundings, with soil moisture and the farmland and urban fractions behind them, and no reflectance band is among its fifteen most influential inputs, so it is not read from the colour of the water at all. Water temperature and dissolved oxygen are meteorologically driven: the day of the year and soil temperature lead, and a predictor given only the calendar and the weather follows a place through time almost as well as the full model.

The portal marks each layer with its kind, because the kind decides what the layer can be used for.

4 · Where, far better than when

The paper looks closely at six monitoring points, each predicted by a model that had not seen it, against the full national record (black in the figure). Water temperature follows the seasons at all six (R² 0.61 to 0.97). Chlorophyll-a sits in the right range for each water body but does not follow its rises and falls (R² from below zero to 0.49).

The same holds across all 1,194 points. Following one place through time, R² is 0.89 for water temperature and 0.50 for dissolved oxygen, but only 0.09 to 0.21 for the other four; at 36 % to 78 % of points their predictions do worse than the point's own long-term mean. Those four layers answer “which water bodies run high?” on any clear date. They do not answer “how did this reservoir change this season?”. The six points are in the map's “Go to…” list.

5 · An uncertainty that means something

Each value comes with σ, from 100 passes of the network with random units switched off (Monte Carlo dropout). On its own that spread is too narrow: its 95 % band held only 46 % to 75 % of the held-out observations. One factor per parameter, from 2.0 for water temperature to 6.7 for suspended solids, widens it so that value ± 1.96 σ holds 94 % to 98 %. For suspended solids, chlorophyll-a, total nitrogen and total phosphorus the lower limit falls below zero and is read as zero.

For five parameters a larger σ goes with a larger error (rank correlation 0.20 to 0.62), so σ can be used to filter or weight. Dissolved oxygen is the exception: its σ is unrelated to the error (0.01) and is not a filter.

6 · What a 1 km cell averages

A released cell is the mean of a median of 52 predictions at 30 m. How much those differ inside one cell depends on the parameter: the coefficient of variation is 0.13 for chlorophyll-a and about 0.09 for suspended solids and total phosphorus, where algal patches and sediment plumes leave structure below a kilometre, 0.075 for total nitrogen, and 0.02 for water temperature and dissolved oxygen, which vary smoothly. Streams narrower than about 60 m do not appear at all: at 30 m they are never a clean water pixel.

The account follows Lee, S., Lee, J., Lee, D., and Kim, H., “A satellite-reanalysis deep-learning 1 km gridded inland water quality dataset for South Korea, 2016–2024”, a Data Descriptor under review at Scientific Data. The skill numbers and the two charts are computed for this site from oof_predictions.csv of the record (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23750573), the out-of-fold predictions of the released model; the inputs each network relies on and the spread within a cell are the paper's findings, as its record's README states them. The numbers behind this site

    
The map on this site is not made of pictures. The browser reads the values themselves: only 8,558 cells ever carry a value, so the whole record fits in two small stores, one ordered by date for the map and one by cell for the series. Values are kept as 16-bit numbers, to 0.001 °C or mg/L for water temperature and dissolved oxygen and to 0.03 % of the value for the other four parameters, and the cursor reads them as stored. The record means, seasonal means and counts are computed from the released files and are not part of the product. All of this is for looking, not for analysis: use the GeoTIFF files for any calculation.

  







  

  

    
About the data

    
A 1 km grid of six water quality parameters for the inland waters of mainland South Korea, with one grid for each date from 6 January 2016 to 29 December 2024 on which a cloud-free satellite overpass allowed it, and a calibrated uncertainty for every value.


    
What the product holds

    
For each of 1,012 dates there are six files, one per parameter: water temperature (WT), suspended solids (SS), chlorophyll-a (Chl-a), dissolved oxygen (DO), total nitrogen (TN) and total phosphorus (TP). Each file has two bands, the predictive mean and its calibrated uncertainty σ.

    
The grid has 239,700 cells. Of these, 99,950 lie inside the national boundary (drawn in from the coast), 13,469 contain persistent open water the method can predict, and 8,558 carry a value on at least one date. On a single date a median of 1,182 cells carry a value (a tenth of dates fewer than 38, a tenth more than 3,526), and all six parameters share the same cells. In all the record holds 1,529,676 cell-values per parameter, about 114 dates per predictable water cell: roughly one a month, unevenly spread.


    
How the grids were made

    
    
      
  • Reference measurements. Records of the Water Environment Information System (WEIS) of the National Institute of Environmental Research, quality-controlled, at 1,194 monitoring points in the five major river basin regions.
    • 
      
  • Satellite input. Harmonized Landsat and Sentinel-2 surface reflectance, the S30 (Sentinel-2) product at 30 m: ten bands, cloud, shadow and snow masked, restricted to persistent open water (Global Surface Water Occurrence of 30 % or more) with the shoreline pixel removed.
    • 
      
  • Other inputs. ERA5-Land hourly reanalysis (weather and land-surface state, with precipitation summed over 24, 48 and 72 hours), Copernicus GLO-30 elevation, and the national land cover map as fractions within 500 m and 2 km.
    • 
      
  • Model. One multi-layer perceptron per parameter (63 inputs, hidden layers of 256, 128 and 64 units), trained on 46,933 matchups taken within three days of an overpass. SS, Chl-a, TN and TP are trained on the logarithmic scale.
    • 
      
  • Uncertainty. The spread of 100 Monte Carlo dropout passes, multiplied by one calibration factor per parameter so that the value ± 1.96 σ holds close to 95 % of held-out observations.
    • 
      
  • From 30 m to 1 km. The networks are run on every 30 m water pixel of every scene. A 1 km cell is written as the mean of its 30 m predictions when they cover at least 80 % of the cell's predictable water and number at least five; otherwise it is left empty. A released cell rests on a median of 52 predictions.
    • 
      
  • Nothing is gap-filled or smoothed, in the files or on this site. A gap in the water network appears as a gap.
    • 
    


    
Three kinds of layer, and how well each does

    
Four independent analyses in the paper (which inputs each network relies on, what happens when groups of inputs are removed, how the skill divides between places and dates, and how much the 30 m predictions vary inside a cell) all separate water temperature and dissolved oxygen from the other four layers; two of them, the inputs each network relies on and the division of the skill, also set total nitrogen apart from the three optical layers. The six layers differ in kind, not only in accuracy.

Loading the skill numbers…

      

    

    
Station-held-out five-fold cross-validation over 46,933 matchups, computed for this site from oof_predictions.csv of the record; the numbers equal Tables 5 and 6 of the paper. “At one place through time” removes each monitoring point's own mean before scoring, so it measures only whether the layer follows a place from date to date. These are the scores of the network's single prediction. The grids carry the mean of 100 dropout passes, which scores within 0.04 of them, with one exception: for total nitrogen its R² at one place through time is 0.13, not 0.20.

    
    
      
  • Optical retrievals (SS, Chl-a, TP) are screening layers: for comparing water bodies with one another and for locating elevated conditions across a region. They are at their best when compared with measurements taken close in time to the overpass.
    • 
      
  • The catchment-context estimate (TN) infers nutrient loading from land cover and terrain. Read it as a contrast between water bodies, not as a series at a fixed place.
    • 
      
  • The meteorologically driven layers (WT, DO) can be used as series at a fixed place, but nearly all of that skill comes from the calendar and the weather, not from the satellite.
    • 
    


    
Read this before you use the numbers

    
    
      
  • None of the six replaces a measurement. The dataset offers coverage in space and time that no monitoring network has, with the skill of each layer stated.
    • 
      
  • A date is an overpass, not a day. The series is irregular. Do not place it on a daily axis without deciding what the gaps mean for your purpose.
    • 
      
  • Summer is thin. The monsoon covers the peninsula in cloud when blooms and floods are most frequent; July has the fewest values of any month, and summer values rest on the least training data.
    • 
      
  • 2016 and the first half of 2017 are thin. Only one Sentinel-2 satellite was flying. Start a trend analysis in July 2017, or at least leave out the autumn of 2016.
    • 
      
  • High values are understated. The grids show where a condition was elevated, not how elevated it was. Take the size of an event from in-situ records. The TP layer never exceeds 0.174 mg/L although measurements reach 2.469 mg/L; SS values above the largest measurement (473.3 mg/L) are extrapolations.
    • 
      
  • Four layers say where far better than when (SS, Chl-a, TN, TP): see the table above. To follow a single reservoir through a season, the local record serves better.
    • 
      
  • Reservoirs rest on less training data than rivers. A reservoir point supplied 16 matchups on average, a river point 111, and most of the grid is reservoir.
    • 
      
  • Estuaries. The model was trained on inland fresh water. Around the Yeongsan River estuary in the southwest (near 35° N, 126.5° E) the water is tidal and optically complex and the model extrapolates poorly; mask that area for freshwater studies. No such mask is shipped.
    • 
      
  • Narrow streams are absent. A stream narrower than about 60 m has no clean 30 m water pixel. Monitoring points often sit on a bridge or a bank: when you compare with a point, take a small neighbourhood, not the single cell under the coordinate.
    • 
      
  • The uncertainty. For SS, Chl-a, TN and TP read the lower limit of value ± 1.96 σ as zero where it falls below zero (the record's metadata gives multipliers for a properly asymmetric interval, which this site uses in its charts). The σ of dissolved oxygen is not related to its error and is not a filter; in summer and at reservoirs its interval is somewhat too narrow.
    • 
      
  • Jeju Island is outside the domain, by design.
    • 
    


    
What this site adds, and what it does not

    
The “Date” view shows the released grids as they are. “Month”, “Seasonal cycle” and “Record mean” are averages over the dates with a value at each cell, computed for this site; they inherit the uneven sampling above, so a record mean leans toward the well-sampled spring and late autumn. Seen from afar the cells are drawn as dots larger than a kilometre so that rivers remain visible; zoom in to see the cells themselves. The monitoring points are shown at the coordinates the network publishes.
